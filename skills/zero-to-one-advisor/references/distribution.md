# Distribution — Sales Is Half the Product

The book's blunt claim: superior sales and distribution can create a monopoly
even without product differentiation, but the reverse is false — a great
product with no distribution channel is a failed business. Engineers
underrate this because sales works best when hidden (nobody thinks of great
salespeople as salespeople). Treat "if we build it, they will come" as the
single most common fatal assumption.

## The two numbers that gate everything

- **CAC** — total cost to acquire one customer through a given channel.
- **CLV** — total net profit earned from that customer over the
  relationship.

A channel only works if **CLV substantially exceeds CAC**. Higher price
points justify (and require) more expensive, higher-touch sales motions.
Always compute or estimate both before endorsing a go-to-market plan; if the
founder can't estimate either, that is the first homework assignment.

## The distribution spectrum

Match the sales motion to the deal size:

| Deal size (per customer) | Motion | Mechanics |
|---|---|---|
| ~$1M+ | **Complex sales** | Founder/CEO sells personally; months-long deals; a few deals a year; buyers are executives and institutions. 50–100% annual growth is the realistic ceiling. |
| ~$10k–$100k | **Personal sales** | A sales team with a repeatable playbook; the challenge is process design, not individual heroics. |
| **~$100–$1k — the dead zone** | **No viable channel** | Too expensive to sell each unit in person; too niche/considered for mass advertising to convert. Many small-business-targeted products die precisely here. Escaping requires moving upmarket, going self-serve/viral, or finding an aggregator. |
| ~$1–$100 | **Marketing & advertising** | Works for cheap, mass-appeal products where broad ads convert; loses to incumbents in any bidding war unless the product is differentiated. |
| ~pennies–$1 (per acquisition) | **Viral** | The product's core use must itself recruit new users (PayPal: getting paid required an account; money as the referral incentive). "We'll add sharing later" is not viral design. |

**Advisor moves:**

- Place the product on this table by its actual price point and say which
  row it lands in. If it's the dead zone, say "dead zone" explicitly and
  treat it as a seven-questions fail, not a marketing to-do.
- **Power law of distribution: one channel.** One channel made to work beats
  a portfolio of half-tried channels. If the founder lists four channels,
  make them pick the one with the best CLV:CAC and defer the rest.

## Beyond customers

Distribution isn't only selling to users:

- **Sell to employees and investors too.** Recruiting is sales; fundraising
  is sales. A company with no distribution ability usually can't hire or
  raise either.
- **Media matters even without ads.** Press coverage reaches not just
  customers but the investors and engineers the company also needs. "We
  ignore PR" is usually a distribution blind spot, not purity.
- **Everybody sells.** A founder who is visibly uncomfortable with selling
  should be told the job includes it; there is no company where "the product
  speaks for itself."
